# The Living Portfolio Zine

## Creative thesis

One dark editorial world, interrupted by four extraordinary objects.

This is a premium portfolio with punk materiality—not a normal portfolio with
zine decorations. The work stays instantly legible. The rebellion lives in the
cropping, physical artifacts, sharp type, cherry chrome, and moments where an
object refuses its grid.

The site should feel authored by a person who builds software, studies biology,
makes games, and writes poetry. It should not feel like four themed websites
sharing a navigation bar.

## The central rule

Every screen gets one dominant idea.

- Hero: one cherry-chrome identity object.
- HEARTLINE: one floating phone with the product alive inside it.
- CancerShift: one beautiful external-validation map.
- To Ash Again: one atmospheric game window breaking its frame.
- Flooded: one physical book or page object.
- About: one human scrapbook, edited as carefully as a magazine profile.
- Contact: one stripped-back closing poster.

If a second visual idea competes for first attention, remove it.

## Keep / kill

Keep:

- black, cream, and cherry;
- paper, tape, patches, pins, handwriting, halftone, and photocopy grain;
- dramatic scale shifts and decisive cropping;
- real interfaces, research evidence, game art, book material, and photographs;
- rare, tactile interactions;
- a few cinematic transitions with complete static fallbacks.

Kill:

- the album tracklist as the site-wide information architecture;
- newspaper columns used merely as decoration;
- department/world title screens;
- game-menu navigation outside the game case study;
- uniform project cards;
- generic torn-paper boxes around ordinary copy;
- equal visual weight for every project;
- constant wobble, rotation, parallax, grain, or hover tricks;
- explanatory copy about the metaphor.

## Homepage hierarchy

The homepage contains four flagship encounters, in this order:

1. HEARTLINE
2. CancerShift
3. To Ash Again
4. Flooded

Taaqat, Nivala, A17, BioLitGraph, Ichthyology, and Student Poets Association

not become six more feature sections.

## Visual system

### Type

- Display: expressive contemporary serif, used at editorial scale.
- Body/UI: neutral grotesque with excellent small-size legibility.
- Evidence: mono only for labels, methods, repository metadata, and numbers.
- Maximum two type families in production; mono may be a sibling face.
- Cutout lettering is an occasional image treatment, never the default heading.

### Color

- Ink: near-black, not a flat void.
- Paper: warm cream.
- Cherry: the identity color and primary interactive accent.
- One muted secondary accent may enter inside each flagship's evidence layer.
- No rainbow discipline coding across the navigation.

### Grid and rhythm

- Desktop: a strict twelve-column editorial grid; artifacts may cross columns,
  text may not.
- Mobile: a newly composed four-column layout, not a shrunken desktop canvas.
- Long copy width: 55–68 characters.
- Each section needs a clear focal object, a short proof rail, and real breathing
  room. Empty space must create tension, not look unfinished.

### Texture

- Texture never sits behind body text.
- One material family per viewport: paper, fabric, photocopy, or chrome—not all.
- Grain is subtle and resolution-independent where possible.
- Tape, stickers, and patches must attach, label, repair, or reveal something.
  They cannot be filler.

### Motion

- Motion reveals material behavior or product logic.
- The hero object may respond slowly to pointer/scroll, never chase the cursor.
- One transition per flagship is enough.
- No information depends on animation.
- Reduced motion receives the same composition as a finished still.
- 3D has a static poster fallback and may be disabled on constrained devices.

## Copy discipline

- Hero identity: at most 14 words.
- Hero support: at most 18 words.
- Homepage project sentence: at most 20 words.
- Proof rail: at most three verified facts.
- One primary action per project.
- No paragraph where one sharp sentence works.
- No “passionate developer,” “intersection of disciplines,” “explore my work,”
  “innovative,” “complex,” “impactful,” or self-awarded superlatives.
- Every claim contains a fact, a consequence, a defensible opinion, or
  personality.

Primary action: `See selected work`

## Ten-second test

A first-time visitor must be able to answer, without scrolling through a menu:

- Who is Hasan?
- What does he do?
- What are the strongest pieces of work?
- What makes at least one of them impressive?
- Where are the résumé and contact links?

If the art direction obstructs any answer, the composition fails.
